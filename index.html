02 · Problem Statement

The Clinical Problem

An abdominal hernia occurs when internal organs or fatty tissue protrude through a weakness in the abdominal wall musculature — one of the most prevalent surgical conditions globally, with over 20 million repair operations annually. Incisional hernia affects 10–15% of all patients who undergo abdominal surgery.

Primary suture repair carries a recurrence rate of 25–63% over 10 years. Synthetic polypropylene meshes reduced this below 10%, but introduced long-term complications because they are permanent and non-degradable:

  • Chronic foreign body inflammation — persistent macrophage activation causing fibrotic encapsulation and chronic pain in 10–30% of patients
  • Mesh shrinkage — polypropylene contracts 20–40% after implantation, distorting local anatomy and increasing recurrence
  • Mesh erosion — rigid mesh can erode into adjacent bowel, bladder, or blood vessels, requiring emergency reoperation
  • Infection and biofilm — infected permanent mesh must be fully surgically removed; cannot be sterilised in-situ
  • Reoperation difficulty — permanent mesh creates dense adhesions complicating all future abdominal surgery

Polymeric Materials — PCL selection, synthesis & characterisation plan
▾

Polycaprolactone (PCL) is selected as the polymer for this mesh design. PCL is a semi-crystalline aliphatic polyester synthesised by ring-opening polymerisation (ROP) of epsilon-caprolactone monomer, with FDA clearance for use in biomedical devices including sutures and drug delivery systems.

The selection is justified on five key grounds: (1) degradation over 12–24 months aligns with the abdominal wall remodelling window; (2) Tg = −60°C makes it highly flexible and elastomeric at body temperature, reducing interfacial shear stress; (3) neutral degradation products (6-hydroxyhexanoic acid) avoid acidic by-products near the peritoneum; (4) reliable electrospinnability from acetone solution; (5) established FDA regulatory precedent. The synthesis route is ROP using stannous octoate at 130°C under nitrogen, producing Mn 60,000–80,000 g/mol with PDI <1.5.

Soft Tissue Replacement — Implant type, placement & fixation strategy
▾

The implant is designed as an electrospun PCL fibrous mesh for treating ventral and incisional hernia (EHS grade W2, defect 4–10 cm). A flat, porous, electrospun PCL mesh is selected as the implant form factor because it simultaneously provides ECM-mimetic architecture for fibroblast-guided tissue regeneration, adequate planar tensile strength, tunable porosity for cellular ingrowth, biodegradability, and conformability to the curved abdominal surface.

MethodAdvantagesLimitations
Suture RepairSimple; no implant; low costRecurrence 25–63%; inadequate for defects >2 cm
Polypropylene MeshLow recurrence; strong; well-studiedPermanent; chronic pain; shrinkage; erosion; infection
PCL Biodegradable MeshBiodegradable; flexible; promotes tissue regeneration; no permanent materialRequires design validation before clinical use
Biologic MeshNatural ECM composition; biocompatibleVery high cost; batch variability; xenogeneic immune risk

The mesh is placed in the retromuscular sublay position (Rives-Stoppa) — between the posterior rectus sheath and the rectus abdominis muscle. Intra-abdominal pressure presses the mesh against the posterior sheath in a self-reinforcing manner. The mesh is completely covered by muscle and fascia on both surfaces, minimising wound complications and direct mesh-viscera contact.

Fixation: absorbable transfascial sutures (PLGA/Vicryl, degrading 60–90 days) at 1 cm intervals around the periphery, plus absorbable PLGA helical tackers at the mesh centre to prevent central bulging. Mesh extends ≥3 cm beyond defect margins on all sides (European Hernia Society guidelines). No permanent hardware remains.

Scaffold Design & Tissue Interaction — Architecture, cell study & sterilisation
▾

The scaffold is fabricated by electrospinning — 15–20 kV applied between a PCL/acetone syringe and a rotating collector, stretching the polymer jet into 1–5 µm fibres deposited as a non-woven mat. Electrospinning is chosen because it uniquely produces a fibrous architecture mimicking native abdominal fascia ECM, guiding fibroblast attachment, alignment, and collagen I synthesis. PCL concentration of 12–15% w/v is optimal: below 10% causes bead defects; above 20% fibres are too thick and porosity drops. A rotating mandrel collector creates partial fibre alignment matching abdominal wall anisotropic loads.

Sterilisation: Ethylene oxide (EtO) at 37°C — below PCL's melting point (55–60°C), achieving SAL 10⁻⁶ per ISO 11135. Steam autoclave (121°C) is definitively excluded — it would melt and fuse the PCL fibres, destroying the mesh structure. UV irradiation excluded for terminal sterilisation due to insufficient penetration depth through the 3D porous mesh.

04 · Working Mechanism

How It Works In Vivo

Once implanted, the mesh acts as a temporary biological scaffold in a phased sequence aligned with natural tissue healing. The key principle is synchrony between mesh degradation and tissue regeneration.

Weeks 0–4
Inflammation & Early Healing
Macrophage recruitment; growth factor release; early fibroblast migration to the repair site.
Mesh: Provides immediate mechanical support; pore structure allows inflammatory cell infiltration and early vascular ingrowth.
Weeks 2–8
Fibroblast Infiltration
Fibroblasts migrate along PCL fibres, adhere, proliferate, and begin depositing type I and III collagen within the mesh pores.
Mesh: ECM-mimetic fibre architecture guides fibroblast alignment and active collagen synthesis.
Weeks 8–24
Tissue Maturation
Collagen fibres cross-link into organised fascial connective tissue; new tissue progressively assumes load-bearing function.
Mesh: Begins bulk hydrolytic degradation; mechanical load transfers to maturing neo-tissue.
Months 6–24
Complete Remodelling
Native connective tissue has fully replaced the mesh scaffold; abdominal wall regains structural integrity.
Mesh: Degradation complete; no foreign material remains — durable repair by patient's own tissue.
